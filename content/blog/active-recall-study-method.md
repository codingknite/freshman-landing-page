Rereading and highlighting are the most popular ways to study. They are also among the least effective. The words look familiar, so it *feels* like you know them. Then the exam asks you to produce the answer from nothing, and the familiarity is gone.

## What active recall is

Active recall is testing yourself before you feel ready. Instead of reading about photosynthesis again, you close the book and try to explain it. Every time you drag an answer out of your memory, that memory gets stronger.

Researchers call this the **testing effect**, and it has been studied for decades. Students who practise retrieving information remember far more a week later than students who spend the same time rereading.

## Three easy ways to start

1. **The blank page.** After a lesson, write down everything you remember on a blank sheet. Then check your notes and fill the gaps in a different colour.
2. **Question cards.** Turn each heading in your notes into a question. Answer it out loud before you flip the card.
3. **Past papers, closed book.** Do questions without notes first. Only look things up after you've tried.

## Why it feels worse (and why that's good)

Active recall is uncomfortable. You will get things wrong. That struggle is the signal that learning is happening. If a study session feels easy, you are probably not remembering much from it.
