Most students treat mock exams like a verdict: here is your score, good luck. The real value is in what happens **after** the mock.

## Sit it like the real thing

Set a timer, put your phone in another room and close your notes. If you check answers halfway through, you are practising a different skill from the one you will need on the day.

## Mark every answer, then ask why

For each mark you lost, write down which of these it was:

| Reason | What it looks like | What to do next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Didn't know it | Blank answer or a guess | Relearn the topic, then test yourself on it |
| Knew it, couldn't recall it | "I knew this!" | More active recall and spaced revision |
| Misread the question | Answered something else | Practise underlining command words |
| Ran out of time | Last questions rushed | Timed practice on single sections |

## Turn the results into a plan

Count up the reasons. If most lost marks came from misreading, more content revision won't help. If most came from topics you never learned, you know exactly where to spend your week.

## Repeat, then compare

Sit another mock a week or two later and compare the reasons, not just the score. Watching one category shrink is the best sign your revision is working.
