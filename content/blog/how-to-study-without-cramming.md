Cramming works just well enough to be tempting. You can hold a lot in your head for a day, but most of it is gone a week later, and exams rarely line up with the one night you stayed up.

The fix is not studying *more*. It is studying **earlier and in smaller pieces**.

## Week 1: Map everything

Write down every topic that could come up. Use your syllabus, your teacher's slides and past papers. Then mark each one:

- **Green**: you could explain it to a friend right now
- **Amber**: you sort of get it
- **Red**: no idea

This list is your plan. Most students skip it and end up revising what they already know, because it feels good.

## Week 2: Learn the red topics

Spend 25 to 40 minutes a day on one red topic. At the end of each session, close your notes and write down everything you remember. Then check what you missed.

That last step matters most. Pulling information out of your head is what makes it stay there.

## Week 3: Mix it up

Now rotate through amber and red topics, and go back to week 2's topics for a quick check. Mixing subjects feels harder than doing one thing at a time. That is the point: the effort is what builds memory.

## Week 4: Practise like it's the real thing

Do at least one full mock exam under timed conditions. Mark it honestly, then spend the rest of the week on whatever lost you the most marks.

The night before, stop early. A quick review and a full night's sleep will do more for you than three extra hours of panic.
